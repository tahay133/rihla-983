Riḥla 983

A pixel-art side-scrolling platformer. An Arab traveller crosses the Islamic world in 983 CE, from Samanid Bukhara through Abbasid Baghdad, Fatimid Cairo and Qayrawan, to Umayyad Córdoba. Each level ends with a real object from the Metropolitan Museum of Art, shown in pixel form with a museum card. Along the way, the political map splits from one caliph into three.

2 · Progression at a glance

Each item unlocks an ability that the next levels need, so the objects are part of how you play and not just collectibles. Banner colours make the politics readable at a glance: Abbasid black, then Fatimid white, then Córdoba red.

4QayrawanFatimid homeland, governed by the Zirids in the Fatimid caliph's name whiteFolio from the Blue Qur'anGold Light: lights dark halls
5CórdobaUmayyad caliph Hishām II; al-Manṣūr holds real power red*Ivory pyxis from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān III's palaceJourney complete: the vitrine ending

*Older sources give the Umayyads white. Recent scholarship (Maribel Fierro) links the Andalusi Umayyads with red. Red also keeps the three caliphates visually distinct, so the game uses it.

Narrative twist for Level 5: the Robe of Honor, a Fatimid allegiance garment, works in Cairo and Qayrawan. In Umayyad Córdoba, guards recognise it as a rival caliph's cloth and give chase. The player feels the rivalry between the caliphates.

3 · Tech spec

Rendering

  • Internal canvas 320×180, scaled by the largest integer that fits the window and letterboxed.
  • ctx.imageSmoothingEnabled = false, CSS image-rendering: pixelated, camera position rounded to whole pixels.
  • Sprites are pre-rendered from palette strings to offscreen canvases. Facing left uses a pre-flipped copy.
  • Three parallax layers per city: far (0.2×) is the handoff backdrop drawn at 160×90 and scaled ×2; mid is 0.5× and near is 0.8×, built from each level's landmark list.

Game loop & physics

  • Fixed 60 Hz update with an accumulator; render on requestAnimationFrame.
  • 16×16 tiles, AABB vs tilemap, one-way platforms, moving platforms carry the player.
  • Player: run accel/decel, variable-height jump, coyote time 6 frames, jump buffer 6 frames.
  • Damage = knockback + 1 heart, with checkpoints. Start with 3 hearts; nobody dies permanently.

Controls

  • Move: ←/→ or A/D · Jump: Z, Space, or ↑ · Action: X
  • Enter: confirm / close card · Esc: pause · M: mute · F3: debug
  • Gamepad API (standard mapping). Touch buttons are optional (commit 14 or later).

State machine

  • Title → Route Map → Level n → Museum Card → Route Map → … → Vitrine ending → Credits.
  • Save progress and unlocks in localStorage, wrapped in try/catch; the game must still run if storage is blocked. Include a "Reset journey" option.
  • Level data are string-row tilemaps (legend below) in src/levels/.

Tilemap legend (suggested)

4 · Art direction

The look is chunky 16-bit, warm earth tones, and one strong landmark silhouette per city. A player should be able to name the city from a screenshot. The item sprites below are the canonical pixel versions of the five Met objects; their source code is in #art-code.

Shared item & character palette

Sprites (shown ×6–×8)

Sizes: traveller 12×16; items 16×16 (pyxis 12×16, flat objects 16×12). In the world, items bob 1 px on a 40-frame cycle and sparkle. On the museum card they're drawn at ×8.

5 · Levels

Each preview is the far-parallax layer, drawn by BACKDROPS[name] at 160×90 and shown here scaled up. "Background cues" must be readable in the finished level. "Do not draw" items are anachronisms for 983.

6 · Items & museum cards

When the traveller touches the item: freeze the game, play a short chime, then slide in the museum card. The card shows the sprite ×8, title, date, place, Met accession, a link to the Met page, where to see it, three facts (or cardText), and the unlock. Enter closes it and returns to the route map.

9 · Acceptance checklist

  • Loads on GitHub Pages with zero console errors; holds 60 fps on a mid-range laptop.
  • Pixels stay crisp at every window size; no sub-pixel jitter while scrolling.
  • All 5 levels can be completed in order. Each takes 2–4 minutes for a new player.
  • Each unlock is required in a later level and works as described.
  • Screenshot test: someone who sees only the background can match it to its city using the cues listed.
  • Every museum card links to the correct Met page and is readable at 1× scale (min ~8 px font at 320×180).
  • Nothing from a "Do not draw" list appears anywhere.
  • Credits list the Met objects, sources, and the artistic-license notes.

10 · Accuracy guardrails

  • The year is 983. Anything built later is out: Timurid blue domes in Samarkand, Mamluk and Ottoman minarets, the Alhambra, the cathedral inside Córdoba's mosque, and al-Manṣūr's 987 extension.
  • Baghdad's Green Dome over the caliph's palace collapsed in 941. Don't draw it intact.
  • Spiral minarets: Samarra's spiral minaret (completed 851) is not in Baghdad. Show it only as a distant labelled silhouette (artistic license). Cairo's Ibn Ṭūlūn spiral is a rebuild of 1296, so leave it out of Cairo.
  • Banners: Abbasid black and Fatimid white are well attested. The Córdoba red is a design choice backed by recent scholarship (see note in section 2).
  • Objects: pixel sprites are simplified homages. Don't embed Met photographs; link to the object pages instead.
  • Simplifications to disclose in credits: the Blue Qur'an sprite shows 5 gold lines (the real page has 15); the camel bowl sprite shows one camel (the real bowl has four).
  • Anything not in this file: leave a // TODO(history): comment. Don't invent.
